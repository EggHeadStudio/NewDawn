# EggHeadStudio: NewDawn — Complete game vision and creative contract

**Date:** 2026-11-27. **Status:** permanent product vision, NOT proof of implementation. **Authoritative companions:** `IDEA.md` (master vision), `VISUAL_ART_BIBLE.md` v2.0 + v2.1 (visual contract), `MASTER_ROADMAP.md`, `IMPLEMENTATION_TRACKER.md`, `DEVELOPMENT_STATUS.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `ASSET_CATALOG.md`, `TEST_REPORT.md`. The user-approved details added during the later discussion are preserved here and in `FEATURE_REQUIREMENTS.md`.

## The game we are building

NewDawn is a genuinely huge Earth-scale, offline-first, third-person, grounded-realism survival and civilization sandbox. Humanity's advanced infrastructure collapsed after a mysterious alien invasion; decades later, nature has reclaimed former roads, cities, ports, factories, farms, industrial zones and airports. Human survivors live in camps, rebuilt homes and autonomous settlements; some possess modern remnants and knowledge while others have only improvised tools. Alien remnants, damaged technology and unexplained sky phenomena persist. The player's life is not scripted. The player can explore, rebuild, hunt, trade, farm, invent, manufacture, drive, sail, fly, lead a village, govern or simply live in a cabin. Good memories come from systemic events, not forced linear quests.

### Non-negotiable aesthetic decision

**Grounded Realism / Reclaimed Earth** is the final style direction, replacing the older early-design phrase *premium stylized*. Realtime visuals must feel naturalistic and visually coherent on both high-end and accessible hardware. Rich PBR ground, varied believable trees with bark/branches/leaves, anatomically recognizable animals, correctly proportioned modern survivors, credible weathered buildings, physically coherent motion and restrained cinematic lighting are essential. Geometry optimization is welcome, obvious primitive cartoons are not accepted as final foreground art. Local CC0/licensed assets may be combined with high-quality procedural geometry. Every asset must have provenance and be available offline when required. A performance compromise must change LOD and rendering cost rather than silently change the whole game into a different art style.

## Player freedom and daily-life loops

A new player can choose guided introduction or unrestricted exploration. The introduction must follow **real existing actions**, be skippable/restartable, persist its progress, work by touch and keyboard, and never block normal play because an obsolete Shelter prefab is gone. Objectives lead through gathering, Pack/equipment, modular building and interacting with Mira; later add water, food, map, settlement, crafting, weather and safety naturally when those systems exist. No fixed medieval-era progression or unrepairable locked modern technology: salvage and repair offer alternative paths to starting over with sticks, but complete manufacturing remains demanding.

Everything appropriate should be physically interactable: inspect, collect, hold in left/right hand, carry, drop, throw, transfer, store, combine, craft, use, equip, repair, refuel, ignite, extinguish, operate, build, paint, dismantle, mount, ride, drive and salvage. The same rules should cover simple objects and later workstations and vehicles. Small items must have consistent mass, collisions, ownership and storage capacity. Unavailable actions display why instead of a fake button.

## World and exploration

The world spans an Earth-like 6,371-km-radius planet. Use canonical spherical geography, geodesic movement and headings, latitude/longitude, pole-safe transforms, local tangent frames, scene-origin rebasing, physical scale and deterministic world generation. A true spherical simulation is the desired end state; **current terrain/render/collision are NOT fully unified**. Streaming includes close interactive terrain, medium region terrain and far landscapes/mountains with bounded caches, worker prioritization, persistent edits, LOD seam stitching and mobile budgets. The actual planet contains tectonically credible landmass shapes, oceans, islands, rivers, lakes, glaciers, mountains, caves, wetlands, beaches, deserts, alpine zones, northern/southern polar regions and volcanic landscapes, rather than repeating a tiny starter island indefinitely.

Climate derives from latitude, altitude, rainfall, ocean influence, seasons and local conditions. Four seasons behave differently between hemispheres. Snow, drought, wind, rain, storms, cloud cover and temperature influence vegetation, wildlife, light, water, survival and travel. The player can landscape ground, dig, raise/lower, smooth, flatten, construct roads and foundations, and preserve those changes in the world.

The **World Atlas** must truthfully represent the generated geography and discoveries. From planetary to local views it supports exploration fog, markers, recorded resources, wildlife, towns, routes and transport estimates. Only display actual political borders or settlement information when the corresponding gameplay state exists. A discoverable journal, environmental stories and photo mode can capture unique places and world events.

## Nature that feels alive

Forests must have recognizable age structure: seedlings, young trees, adult and ancient trees with branch shapes, true foliage/needles, trunks, bark, roots, dead timber, undergrowth and open clearings. Distinct pine, spruce, birch, oak, maple, palm, acacia and tropical broadleaf families belong in suitable regions, with more regional flora later. Realistic rocks, ore deposits, moss, ferns, grasses, flowers, shrubs, cactus, wetland reeds and soil/leaf litter make terrain credible. Subtle wind drives trees, leaves, grass, smoke, clouds and water consistently. Dense visuals must never permanently hide the player; selective geometry-aware foliage clearance is preferred over fading whole forests.

Animals include **deer, rabbit, wolf, bird, brown bear, snake, squirrel, reindeer, moose, cow, sheep and fox** as the initial 12 targets. All ultimately need believable authored/high-quality meshes, proportionate sizes, rigged species-specific motion, habitat and behavior. Their current procedural toys remain incomplete. Later add horse, camel, large cats, arctic animals, tropical wildlife, insects, amphibians and fish, sharks, dolphins, whales, rays and other marine species. Herds, migration, feeding, nesting, predator/prey systems, breeding, hunting, husbandry, taming, mounting and riding are real systems, not animated set dressing. Wild rides can be unpredictable or dangerous; tame animal control increases with trust and equipment.

### Organic-world details from the design discussions — preserve permanently

- Distant skies sometimes show **flocks of birds**, coherent circling groups and **V-shaped migrating geese/cranes**, scaled and animated cheaply at long distances. Species, season, climate and time determine events. These are world ambience, not necessarily nearby fully simulated animals.
- **Flies and other scavenger insects** gather around animal carcasses and fallen bodies where temperature and biome permit. Winter/cold regions, heavy rain and wind reduce or eliminate insect swarms. Bounded particles/audio cues should attract attention without causing mobile overdraw or triggering everywhere.
- **Corpses/carcasses decay** through a persisted ecology lifecycle: fresh body -> visible deterioration/scavenger interest -> skeletal remains -> slowly returning to soil or being cleaned up by gameplay rules. The carcass should not remain a permanent expensive rigid body; stable identity, resource yields, cleanup and chunk streaming matter. This is atmospheric fictional wildlife/survival content, not an instruction for physical harm.
- Gathering, breaking rock, digging and impact should throw **material-appropriate splinters, soil clumps, dust, stone chips, foliage, and where appropriate limited blood effects**, not uniform bright cubes. Use physically motivated emission, pooled particles, impact decals and game settings for intensity.
- Distant birds, insects, smoke, waves, shifting weather, roaming creatures and faint ruins should make the world feel populated even when no NPC is nearby.

## The sky and unusual events

The sky is part of the world, not a static wallpaper. At clear night, looking up should reveal a discoverable **moon with a changing orbit/phase**, rich stars and an aesthetically compelling **Milky Way/galactic band**. Cloud cover, moonlight, season, latitude and time affect visibility. Clear sunrise/sunset should have natural atmospheric depth; storms should feel turbulent and cloud layers should be convincingly lit. The player must be able to look upward with a usable camera, and high-quality sky rendering must not create inaccessible mobile control schemes. Nights can be tense and dark but moonlight, torches and atmospheric adaptation should retain legibility.

**Rare atmospheric events** occasionally add mystery: a remote aircraft crossing high overhead; a distant helicopter; a far-away unidentified craft; an eerie light; a helicopter apparently pursuing an unknown object; sporadic distant alien glows. Events should be infrequent, procedurally seeded/bounded, integrated with weather/time/lore, and avoid constant spectacle. Some may lead to later optional discoverable sites; early implementations must not pretend entire quests exist if only visual ambient cues are implemented. A toggle to reduce surprise/tension is compatible with peaceful play.

## Characters, enemies and movement

The protagonist, Mira, residents, raiders and adversaries must share one believable quality level and correct human scale. Modern/post-apocalyptic layered clothing, trousers, workwear, rainwear, utility belts, sturdy boots and functional visible backpacks are central. Fantasy Quaternius outfits are transitional placeholders, not finished modern gear. Customization affects actual model/preview/gameplay/save consistently (body, stature, face, hair, facial hair, clothing, equipment, identity). Swimming, climbing, carrying, mining, chopping, crafting, driving and combat require context-correct animation. Weapons and tools appear in actual hands and make contact at the relevant point in the animation.

NPC and enemy animation, collisions and damage must become physically coherent. Prefer a proven bounded rigid-body engine (e.g. Rapier, after an architectural evaluation), ragdoll only for nearby transient entities, deterministic saveable state rather than persisting millions of moving rigid bodies. Enemy types include human raiders, recognizable alien horrors and intentionally reconciled prototype undead/necromancer/night variants. Combat includes melee contact checks, ballistics, throwable items, movement, cover, line of sight, terrain/building obstruction, damage, retreat and meaningful injuries. Events should tell a consistent post-invasion story.

## Construction and engineering

Construction is piece-by-piece and enterable: foundations, floors, beams, walls, corners, windows, glass, operable doors, stairs, platforms, roofs, rooms, furniture, storage, lighting and mechanical/electrical workstations. Materials evolve from timber and scavenged stone through kiln-fired bricks, glass, concrete, steel and advanced materials. Roofs support gable/shed and complex L/T roof geometry rather than hiding unsupported shapes behind permanent fallback. Indoor cameras and walls must preserve character visibility. Weather shelter is a genuine later physical survival mechanic, not only a graphical rain mask.

Build roads (dirt/gravel/stone/modern surfaces) with graceful curves/junctions, grading and vehicle compatibility. Player can dig/raise/flatten/smooth and paint surfaces with real front-face UV brush targeting, interpolation, erase and undo. Concealed building-level power and real generation/storage/distribution should be testable without visible spaghetti wires. Houses can be repaired, destroyed, burned, renovated, connected to utilities and occupied by autonomous people. Fences, gates, watchtowers and traps should protect settlements and integrate with collisions and damage.

Fire can spread through real in-game combustible surfaces based on wind, wetness, distance and fuel, with bounded offscreen approximations. Players can use buckets, fill them at water, extinguish fire, eventually use pumps, hoses and portable extinguishers. Explosions, collapses and larger fictional cinematic destructive events have physical-looking debris/flash/smoke and local consequences; use fictional game mechanics, not real hazardous recipe instructions.

## Discovery, crafting, production and transportation

Real-world-like material logic, but gameplay-safe, drives ore, clay, glass, bricks, metal, wood, mechanical components, circuitry, batteries, machinery, engines and research. A functioning crafting chain produces visible items and unlocks genuinely useful capabilities. Ancient technology and salvaged aircraft are discoverable before the player manufactures high-tech devices. Vehicles range from bicycles, handcarts, wagons, repaired cars and work trucks to boats, trains, aircraft and helicopters, with cargo, fuel, ownership, repairs and actual traversal. Sea routes, rivers, rail tracks and ports are part of the economic world. Chemistry experiments use **abstract fictional representations** for hazardous outputs.

## Living civilizations and multiplayer later

Individuals have lasting names, appearances, personalities, goals, relationships, work, homes, needs and belongings. Populations establish villages and grow without player direction, choose jobs, mine, farm, build, trade, repair and consume real goods; distant settlements keep developing with bounded deterministic simulation. Citizens own wealth and finite goods, prices react to shortages, and economic conservation is maintained. Personal reputation and settlement trust create consequences; the player may become a member, leader, legitimately elected mayor or ruler. Elections, councils, territories, property rights, alliances, diplomacy, disputes, conflicts and peace have explainable outcomes and affect the actual world map.

Online multiplayer is a future optional mode, not current Batch 001. When appropriate, first decouple authoritative simulation from Three.js and local UI. Only then consider Cloudflare Workers, Durable Objects, WebSocket rooms, synchronization, authentication and abuse controls. No Firebase/Cloudflare credentials belong in committed source.

## Emotional tone: audio, mystery, and peace

Daytime can be meditative and beautiful. Forest breeze, distant wildlife, individual footsteps by surface, wind, ocean, tool hits and machinery make the world audible. At night, less-visible threats may be communicated through spatial sounds, owl/owl-like wildlife, distant machinery or eerie alien resonance. Dynamic original or licensed music increases tension only when warranted, then returns to calm. Menus expose separate sound/music and accessibility controls; browser audio starts only after a user gesture. Music and ambience are offline-compatible and legally usable.

## Performance, camera and user experience

The game runs in Chrome/Safari/Edge as a Vite/Three.js/PWA, on laptop/desktop and **older or mid-range phones**, not only a flagship iPhone. Use bounded memory, reasonable offline package sizes, GPU-friendly materials, instancing, real LOD, texture compression, lazy regions, object pooling, quality presets and dynamic resolution when measured to help. Optional **cinematic depth of field** should be subtle and disabled on low-end/mobile by default. Blur does NOT reduce the cost of complex trees; optimize source geometry/overdraw first. Maintain the selected player's comfort settings, avoid aggressive adaptation/popping, keep UI sharp and legible.

Mobile must preserve **always-available bottom-left joystick and four-way right-side Jump/A/B/Weapon controls**, safe-area spacing, simultaneous touches and compact tool hub. Desktop needs intuitive WASD/mouse camera, reasonable menus and gamepad consideration. All menus need keyboard/touch access, Finnish and English eventually. Photo Mode can offer controlled depth of field and cinematic framing independently from gameplay.

## Product integrity

No false 'DONE' assertions. A feature is only verified after real tests, relevant gameplay screenshots and explicit limitations. Physical-device iOS/Android performance and controls must be verified before consumer release. Keep old saves loadable where reasonable, explicitly back up engineering saves for unavoidable schema migrations, and protect source assets and security credentials. The user decides **when** to commit, push, merge, publish or change infrastructure. Agents can implement within an approved milestone autonomously but may not make consequential external writes without review.
