# Architecture

- `src/main.ts` still owns scene orchestration, browser controls, UI and IndexedDB transactions. Gameplay updates use a 30 Hz accumulator with capped elapsed time, but simulation is still coupled to Three.js objects, input, camera and UI; this is not yet an authoritative server-ready simulation.
- `src/planet.ts` maps shared x/z gameplay coordinates onto an Earth-scale equirectangular meter domain (about 40,030,176 by 20,015,088 meters), with periodic longitude, reflected polar coordinates, equivalent-position projection, canonical latitude/longitude conversions, geodesic distances and great-circle actor steps. Player and rover movement uses transported headings; Mira and wildlife use geodesic movement/proximity while retaining projected compatibility coordinates. Terrain, structures, collision and rendering geometry remain flat projected.
- `src/world.ts` retains the original starter terrain and resource layout, blends into generated terrain beyond it, incrementally loads 24-unit chunks, unloads and disposes distant chunks, and persists terrain edits and depleted nodes by normalized coordinate. Loaded chunks form a bounded cache; ground cover is instanced, while trees and resource props are not. Vegetation family selection now uses biome/latitude to produce deciduous trees, conifers and sparse desert cactus; it remains a limited procedural set. Periodic coordinate rebasing limits rendering precision loss.
- `src/atlas.ts` provides a per-seed, bounded one-degree biome raster sampled from the same `biomeAt` terrain source used by streamed world generation. `main.ts` draws the raster only within explored atlas tiles. This shows generated base biomes, not live terrain edits, and the map remains coarse.
- `src/appearance.ts` defines serializable appearances; `src/characters.ts` provides the procedural fallback. `src/authored-character.ts` loads local Quaternius glTF prototypes and clips asynchronously, clones skinned scenes using `SkeletonUtils`, and runs per-instance animation mixers. The player, Mira, raiders and `src/character-preview.ts` use this path; the other enemies retain the procedural hierarchy in `src/enemies.ts`. Shared prototype geometry and textures remain cached, while cloned skeletons and tinted materials are disposed per character. Separate head, outfit and hair skeletons are animated in parallel. There is no character LOD; not all saved appearance values have matching authored assets.
- `src/wildlife.ts` holds serializable animal state and simulation; `src/animal-assets.ts` creates and animates the corresponding procedural models. Population and simulation remain island-wide rather than chunk-based.
- `src/camera.ts` contains camera-relative movement, angle interpolation and zoom bounds; pointer-lock mouse-look, fallback right-drag, camera and input orchestration still live in `main.ts`.
- `src/gameplay.ts` contains inventory costs, crafting, building checks and day/night helpers.
- `src/economy.ts` defines deposit types and mineral inventory; `src/world.ts` generates finite three-hit deposits independently of legacy resource nodes, persists per-deposit progress by planetary coordinate and excludes existing saved construction. `src/smelting.ts` defines atomic fuel/input reservation and timed metal and glass production; workstation state and the iron-pickaxe upgrade are orchestrated in `main.ts`. Other workstations, technology and most uses for the materials are not implemented.
- `src/construction.ts` defines sparse wooden structural records, grid snapping, exposed-floor-edge suggestions, occupancy, atomic wall/frame replacement, costs/refunds, door/window collision, floor/stair surfaces and bounded perimeter flood-fill. `src/construction-render.ts` batches pieces in instanced meshes and handles door animation, translucent glass and per-roof visibility. Placement, glass consumption, room reconciliation, persistence and simple player/NPC/rover collision are still orchestrated in `main.ts`. Structures are currently stored in one save array and rendered globally, not streamed by chunk; stair collision is approximate and there is no structural support simulation.
- `src/roof.ts` partitions bounded enclosed cells at wall boundaries, plans deterministic flat, rectangular gable or single-slope tile profiles, and reconciles paid automatic roofs against manual roofs and upper floors. Irregular gables fall back to a continuous single slope; incompatible neighboring or upper structures restrict pitching. Explicit reshaping reuses paid tiles without converting legacy/manual roofs automatically. `main.ts` persists pending elevation/style requests and retries on inventory changes, not every frame. `src/shelter.ts` rebuilds a cell-indexed room and overhead-surface cache on construction refresh; `src/effects.ts` masks individual rain/snow particles below those surfaces. This is visual coverage, not thermal or survival simulation. `src/construction-render.ts` batches saved pitch profiles, creates sloped surfaces and boundary end caps, and fades the occupied room when the camera is above.
- `src/npc.ts` contains the survivor's basic activity selection and movement helper; scene-level task execution is still in `main.ts`.
- `src/power.ts` computes distance-linked electrical components, wind generation, battery storage and lamp consumption.
- `src/calendar.ts` supplies deterministic season and daily weather selection; `src/effects.ts` renders lightweight particles.
- `src/environment-lighting.ts` calculates continuous daylight and weather-dependent moonlight; `main.ts` applies the results to Three.js lights, sky and fog. `src/fire.ts` owns serializable handheld-torch transitions, crafting and nearby fixture selection; `src/fire-render.ts` attaches the torch to the authored or fallback hand and renders flames, a bounded spark pool and local light. Four shared point lights illuminate nearby posts. Post state remains in the global building array: distant post meshes are hidden but are not yet chunk-unloaded. Fire does not consume fuel over time or respond to weather.
- `src/construction-occlusion.ts` identifies instanced wall and frame geometry between camera and character; individual obstructing pieces fade. `src/tree-occlusion.ts` checks several character-height sightlines against nearby trees. Enclosed rooms shorten the follow-camera distance. This does not constitute complete camera or building-geometry acceptance.
- `src/save.ts` validates version 1–12 save shapes before loading. Older versions receive browser-local backups before migration; version 11 adds Atlas state, and version 12 persists canonical geographic player/vehicle positions that must agree with projected save coordinates within one meter. Version-12 saves may also include Mira's canonical position; wildlife state may include canonical actor positions, with consistency validation. Older saves derive missing geography from projected coordinates. Main-menu JSON export and validated import are available; import backs up the replaced primary slot. Browser I/O remains in `main.ts`; enemies and projectiles are transient.
- `src/pwa.ts` handles optional installation and user-approved update activation. `vite.config.ts` emits a versioned service worker which precaches a matching shell, hashed assets and bundled character models/textures; saves remain in IndexedDB. The character files add approximately 20 MB to the precache. Multi-tab coordination is not yet implemented.
- `src/geography.ts` defines Earth-radius and equatorial-circumference constants, latitude/longitude normalization, haversine distance, great-circle bearing and forward movement, ECEF/tangent conversions, parallel transport of headings, hierarchical tile keys, climate zones and hemispheric season helpers. `main.ts` uses geographic movement for the player, rover and Mira; `src/wildlife.ts` uses it for animal movement and proximity. `src/planet.ts` adapts projected compatibility positions to canonical geography. A local-tangent frame is not yet integrated across entities, terrain and rendering.
- `main.ts` keeps gameplay/save coordinates unwrapped and applies a 1,024-meter scene-root render origin. Camera and terrain/build placement rays account for the origin; this is a rendering precision mitigation, not a fully separated global/local/render transform architecture.
- The World Atlas is rendered by `main.ts` on a procedural canvas. M or the Atlas quick control opens it; it uses hierarchical equirectangular scale levels, persistent compact explored-tile IDs, named map markers and straight-line distance/transport estimates. Atlas state is optional in version-11-and-later saves and remains render-compatible with older saves.
- `src/style.css` styles the interface; `public/` contains the PWA shell; `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` deploys the built Pages artifact.

This is not yet a multiplayer-ready simulation boundary or a complete spherical planet. Entity state, distant NPC/wildlife simulation, seam-aware navigation and polar gameplay require further separation and verification.

- `src/physics.ts` provides lightweight shared actor-movement and ray-obstruction queries for structural pieces and buildings. It avoids an external rigid-body dependency but does not implement full Rapier-style dynamics. `main.ts` applies it to enemies and projectiles, while preserving the proven legacy player/vehicle collision helpers. Jump, gravity and water immersion state remain in `main.ts`; thrown-stone projectiles use bounded local meshes.
