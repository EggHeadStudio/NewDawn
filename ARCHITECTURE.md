# Architecture

- `src/main.ts` still owns scene orchestration, browser controls, UI and IndexedDB transactions. Gameplay updates use a 30 Hz accumulator with capped elapsed time, but simulation is still coupled to Three.js objects, input, camera and UI; this is not yet an authoritative server-ready simulation.
- Action feedback uses one polite live status inside the active menu, panel or toolbar, positioned in the content flow near the last action; the compact Tools header hosts its guidance so scrolling cannot hide Pack behind the sticky header. Closing its host removes that status. The gameplay toast sits below the minimap on desktop and retains the mobile placement. Messages do not change game or save state.
- The primary-action hint and gathering use the same nearest-resource/deposit selection. During normal play the hint refreshes at most every 0.25 simulated seconds, naming deposits and indicating a missing pickaxe; for trees it identifies the saw requirement, working distance, falling, trunk sawing and remaining cut wood. Desktop Space gives combat priority when a threat is nearby; mobile A retains its gathering behavior. Build and vehicle actions retain their own labels. The hint adds no save state or geometry and is not a complete screen-reader or physical-device acceptance.
- `src/planet.ts` maps shared x/z gameplay coordinates onto an Earth-scale equirectangular meter domain (about 40,030,176 by 20,015,088 meters), with periodic longitude, reflected polar coordinates, equivalent-position projection, canonical latitude/longitude conversions, geodesic distances and great-circle actor steps. Player and rover movement uses transported headings; Mira and wildlife use geodesic movement/proximity while retaining projected compatibility coordinates. Terrain, structures, collision and rendering geometry remain flat projected.
- `src/world.ts` retains the original starter terrain and resource layout, blends into generated terrain beyond it, incrementally loads 24-unit chunks, unloads and disposes distant chunks, and persists terrain edits and depleted nodes by normalized coordinate. Gameplay height samples follow the rendered terrain triangle diagonal, which is mirrored in reflected polar copies; terrain refresh repositions resources, deposits and ground cover. Ground-cover offsets and terrain climate noise respect planetary coordinate copies. Loaded chunks form a bounded cache; ground cover is instanced, while trees and resource props are not. Vegetation family selection now uses biome/latitude to produce deciduous trees, conifers and sparse desert cactus; it remains a limited procedural set. Periodic coordinate rebasing limits rendering precision loss.
- Floor-level structural tiles suppress overlapping decorative grass, ferns and flowers using a chunk-indexed derived mask; upper floors and harvestable resources are unaffected. Original instance transforms are retained per loaded chunk and restored when floors are removed. Placement, demolition, save loading, terrain changes and streaming refresh the mask, including wrapped/reflected projected copies. Suppressed instances remain submitted to the GPU; this is not structural vegetation clearing or persistent environmental simulation.
- Decorative flowers use locally generated vertex-colored, three-head clusters instead of solid polyhedrons. Instances retain the existing deterministic placement, floor masking and seasonal visibility; an independent seeded rotation varies their orientation. Their per-chunk instanced geometry is disposed with the chunk and adds no downloaded asset or save field. Broader understory composition remains incomplete.
- `src/atlas.ts` provides a per-seed, bounded one-degree biome raster sampled from the same `biomeAt` terrain source used by streamed world generation. `main.ts` draws the entire coarse raster without exploration fog; saved exploration IDs remain intact. This shows generated base biomes, not live terrain edits.
- The minimap is north-up like the Atlas: positive projected z is north, and terrain and entity markers use the same orientation. An outlined arrow follows the player's facing, or the rover's heading while driving. It remains a local projected map, not a fully validated polar or seam-aware navigation view.
- `src/appearance.ts` defines serializable appearances; `src/characters.ts` provides the procedural fallback. `src/authored-character.ts` loads local Quaternius glTF prototypes and clips asynchronously, clones skinned scenes using `SkeletonUtils`, and runs per-instance animation mixers. The player, Mira, raiders and `src/character-preview.ts` use this path; the other enemies retain the procedural hierarchy in `src/enemies.ts`. Shared prototype geometry and textures remain cached, while cloned skeletons and tinted materials are disposed per character. Separate head, outfit and hair skeletons are animated in parallel. There is no character LOD; not all saved appearance values have matching authored assets.
- The player and character preview attach a locally bundled, shared backpack prototype to the authored outfit's `spine_02`; a failed load is reported without disabling the character, and disposed avatars cannot receive late attachments. The Pack menu toggles the player's backpack visibility, including before its asynchronous asset loads. Optional `backpackEquipped` in version-13 saves persists the choice; older saves default to worn. This starter cosmetic has no capacity or general item-slot system, and the preview always shows it. Selected hand tools remain visible between actions; required action tools temporarily take precedence and the torch remains on the other hand. Procedural fallback characters do not yet render the backpack.
- `src/wildlife.ts` holds serializable animal state and simulation; prey reacts to nearby living wolves, foxes and bears, and player-damage events identify the attacking species. With collision enabled, predators stop outside the combined target-body radii while remaining able to attack; without it the old 0.9/1.3 m separation remains. `src/wildlife-collision.ts` performs bounded geographic-frame animal/player and animal/world queries, overlap recovery, detours and attack obstruction checks using cached resource and structural descriptors. Player movement keeps its legacy projected building/water checks and animal contact, but does not use static natural-resource collision; the rover is excluded from the new actor collision system. Projected resource density can trap large bodies at high latitudes, and some prey can oscillate after overlap recovery. This is not complete planetary collision or pathfinding. `src/animal-assets.ts` creates procedural fallbacks; `src/authored-animal.ts` shares locally bundled sheep, hare, cow, crow, squirrel, wolf, fox, roe-deer, moose, grizzly-bear and reindeer prototypes across independent animals. Models ease visually toward saved simulation headings, and birds ease into and out of flight. Population and simulation remain island-wide rather than chunk-based.
- `src/rover.ts` builds the procedural vehicle and bounds its steering, wheel rotation and terrain-following body pitch/roll. `main.ts` retains the legacy projected rover checks against buildings, structures and deep water: the attempted tree/animal/player collision integration was disabled after high-latitude vehicles became trapped. Existing entry/exit remains, and an optional saved yaw defaults to zero for older saves. The vehicle has no fuel, cargo, repair or complete collision.
- `src/camera.ts` contains camera-relative movement, angle interpolation, zoom/pitch bounds and follow-orbit/aim separation. Upward requests retain the old minimum eye orbit while raising aim using actual horizontal focus separation; ordinary framing and terrain/building collision remain unchanged. Pointer-lock mouse-look, fallback right-drag, touch and camera orchestration still live in `main.ts`.
- `src/performance-monitor.ts` holds up to 300 recent gameplay frame durations and, on request, up to 18,000 samples for a 30-second gameplay benchmark. Interruptions cancel rather than silently mixing observations; the report separates sustained timings from live renderer/loaded-chunk snapshots. It does not report GPU timing or measured VRAM, and copying is local and user initiated. `src/render-quality.ts` bounds internal pixel ratio by selected quality and coarse-pointer capability without changing CSS or pointer coordinates.
- `src/gameplay.ts` contains inventory costs, crafting, building checks and day/night helpers.
- `src/economy.ts` defines deposit types and mineral inventory; `src/world.ts` generates finite three-hit deposits independently of legacy resource nodes, persists per-deposit progress by planetary coordinate and excludes existing saved construction. `src/smelting.ts` defines atomic fuel/input reservation and timed metal and glass production; workstation state and the iron-pickaxe upgrade are orchestrated in `main.ts`. Other workstations, technology and most uses for the materials are not implemented.
- `src/construction.ts` defines sparse wooden structural records, grid snapping, exposed-floor-edge suggestions, occupancy, atomic wall/frame replacement, costs/refunds, door/window collision, floor/stair surfaces and bounded perimeter flood-fill. `src/construction-render.ts` batches pieces in instanced meshes and handles door animation, translucent glass and per-roof visibility. Placement, glass consumption, room reconciliation, persistence and simple player/NPC/rover collision are still orchestrated in `main.ts`. Structures are currently stored in one save array and rendered globally, not streamed by chunk; stair collision is approximate and there is no structural support simulation.
- Wooden floors, walls, doors and roofs lazily share bundled color/normal maps and retain existing paint and opacity handling; framed walls remain a single instanced geometry. Missing textures report an error and retain a one-pixel fallback. No building records or save format changed. See `ASSET_CATALOG.md` for provenance and the estimated texture cost.
- `src/footprints.ts` renders a bounded pool of 64 terrain-aligned, alternating transient boot marks in one instanced mesh. Snow, sand, wet soil, ordinary soil and rock have different opacity/color and lifetimes; their edges and final five seconds fade. A multiplicative unlit material darkens the already-lit terrain instead of making marks glow in shadows. Marks are not saved and reset on world load. Swimming, driving, airborne movement, blocked gameplay and raised constructed floors do not emit them. This is a visual approximation, not persistent terrain deformation.
- `src/roof.ts` partitions bounded enclosed cells at wall boundaries, plans deterministic flat, rectangular gable or single-slope tile profiles, and reconciles paid automatic roofs against manual roofs and upper floors. New games select gable; saves without a style continue to select flat and existing pieces are not converted. Irregular gables fall back to a continuous single slope; incompatible neighboring or upper structures restrict pitching. Building above a pitched roof requires removing the affected tiles first. Explicit reshaping reuses paid tiles without converting legacy/manual roofs automatically. `main.ts` persists pending elevation/style requests and retries on inventory changes, not every frame. `src/shelter.ts` rebuilds a cell-indexed room and overhead-surface cache on construction refresh; `src/effects.ts` masks individual rain/snow particles below those surfaces. This is visual coverage, not thermal or survival simulation. `src/construction-render.ts` batches saved pitch profiles, creates correctly wound exterior and interior surfaces and boundary end caps, and fades the occupied room when the camera is above. Single-sided roof rendering avoids stacking the front and back faces of a faded roof. Opaque roof instances write depth in a separate pass from faded instances so transparent walls cannot incorrectly cover a solid roof; both passes share the geometry and opacity data, and a second pass is visible only for a mixed batch. Doorway framing can still obscure the player.
- `src/npc.ts` contains the survivor's basic activity selection and movement helper; scene-level task execution is still in `main.ts`.
- `src/power.ts` computes distance-linked electrical components, wind generation, battery storage and lamp consumption.
- `src/calendar.ts` supplies deterministic season and daily weather selection; `src/effects.ts` renders lightweight precipitation. Snow retains soft circular point sprites; rain and storm render fading, wind-tilted line segments from the same 150 bounded particle positions and shelter visibility. Only one precipitation mesh is visible at a time; the extra rain geometry adds no downloaded assets or save data. This does not model snow accumulation or full rainfall.
- `src/sky.ts` derives local-time solar direction from latitude and day and approximates lunar orbit/phase. A camera-centered procedural sky shader draws discs, an initial deterministic starfield and faint galactic band before weather-blended clouds; daylight and moonlight reduce their visibility. Stars use bounded cube-face candidates and angular direction-space distance rather than pole-stretched latitude/longitude distances; they are decorative, not a catalog. The lunar terminator faces the projected sun direction, while the existing approximate phase controls its illuminated fraction. `environmentLighting` supplies artistically amplified directional moonlight and ambient fill for night readability; both are attenuated by weather, phase and moon altitude in the runtime. The fill is global and does not model shelter occlusion. Their broader visual and weather acceptance remains open. The directional lights use the same directions in projected coordinates. Longitude does not offset the shared in-game clock, and fixed-hour enemy-night logic is not yet astronomical.
- With tone mapping enabled, the sky shader compensates for the difference between the tone-mapped horizon and Three.js's unlit fog color, tapering the correction above the horizon. This reduces the contrast at streamed water/terrain boundaries without fading water, altering fog distances or adding geometry. It does not provide distant terrain or remove every horizon seam.
- `src/terrain-material.ts` loads two shared, locally packed atlases for six PBR families, blending canonical-coordinate grass, forest, dirt, sand, rock and snow with explicit texture gradients. Atlas failure is logged and leaves a playable fallback; chunk materials and shared textures have separate disposal ownership. `src/nature-assets.ts` eagerly loads six tree prototypes and demand-loads the mature fir and regional fan palm when their resources appear; `src/ground-nature-assets.ts` loads rock, grass, fern and shrub prototypes. Deterministic subsets of oak-, birch-, pine-, spruce- and fir-classified resources use mature visuals; their ordinary authored visuals remain the fallback while these assets load or if they fail. Failed optional fir and palm requests are retried when the browser reports it is online. The birch's woody branches are distinct from its wind-animated, season-tinted foliage. `src/vegetation-lod.ts` shares near/mid foliage geometry and scales instanced cover counts with quality, distance and hysteresis; Low uses a shorter tree near-detail radius. `src/tree-occlusion.ts` identifies trees across an enlarged crown-aware corridor; authored foliage uses a smoothed shader sightline discard region, while procedural meshes and the narrow palm trunk use smooth per-mesh fade. On multisampled WebGL contexts, alpha-masked tree and ground foliage enable alpha-to-coverage. Existing resource IDs and ownership coordinates survive visual replacement, and failed loads retain fallbacks. Trees and instanced cover use shared GPU wind uniforms. The mature assets and overall forest are not visually accepted; other tree families and cactus art remain incomplete. `src/world.ts` samples terrain depth into the subdivided water surface for shoreline transparency; physical water height remains unchanged. `src/water-effects.ts` preallocates eight short-lived ripple/particle pairs for player entry and swimming. Wildlife and rover disturbances are not implemented. These remain projected-frame effects, not terrain LOD or staged tangent geometry.
- `src/environment-lighting.ts` calculates continuous daylight, weather-dependent moonlight, an artistic weather-attenuated night visibility floor and bounded weather/altitude/quality fog ranges; `main.ts` eases active fog to those ranges. The floor is stronger in overcast weather than the initial starlight-only approximation and can influence dim partial-moon light; it is not physical atmospheric modelling. Directional lights and their targets follow the player/rover in the rebased scene so their direction and shadow focus remain local. `src/fire.ts` owns serializable handheld-torch transitions, crafting and nearby fixture selection; `src/fire-render.ts` attaches the torch to the authored or fallback hand and renders wind-swayed, independently phased shader flames, a bounded spark pool and local light. Sparks use soft circular point masks and per-particle lifetime opacity; the pool remains at most 20 points in one draw. Each flame uses a single four-triangle crossed mesh with a shared animation clock; four shared point lights illuminate nearby posts. Post state remains in the global building array: distant post meshes are hidden but are not yet chunk-unloaded. Fire does not consume fuel over time or respond to weather.
- `scripts/generate-mature-fir.mjs` authors a deterministic whorled fir offline from curved branch tubes and small needle sprays with checksum-verified CC0 photos. Its two-material, 43,452-triangle optional GLB replaces the old sparse sampled fir without changing save identity or cache ownership. A later composition checkpoint increased the deterministic adult subset from 8% to 32% of fir resources, without adding resource nodes. No generator executes in the game and no new runtime dependency is introduced. Spruce now shares the same 32% mature selection, and half of the remaining spruce/fir resources reuse the same cached prototype as a 0.6-scale young fir (deterministic `treeAgeClass()`; sapling fallback until cached). Tree height affects new timber yield; saved fallen/log quantities remain authoritative.
- Authored tree foliage now casts alpha-tested sunlight shadows when the existing quality policy enables shadows. Each foliage mesh has a depth material sharing its cutout textures and pre-clearance wind hook; camera-dependent visible-material clearance deliberately does not clear the sunlight shadow. Resource/chunk disposal releases these per-instance depth materials without releasing shared textures. Existing shadow-map resolution and light-frustum bounds remain; shadow GPU/overdraw cost is not included in the live main-pass renderer snapshot.
- Low sky quality retains the original two-octave cloud field and weather-dependent maximum opacity (0.82 Clear/Cloudy to 1.0 Storm). Medium/High cache a finite-layer, eight-sample cloud approximation with four-octave shape and coarse separate sun/moon lighting in one 256-pixel-per-face RGBA8 cube. Linear premultiplied scattering/opacity composites over live full-resolution sky/discs/stars. A six-face refresh happens at most once per update and five times per simulated second, without catch-up; no depth/mipmaps are allocated (1.5 MiB estimated color storage). `Sky` owns the target/materials and shares its sphere geometry between main/cache scenes; Low releases the target, re-enable/context restoration invalidates it, and disposal removes the renderer listener and owned resources. This is directional sky art, not traversable volumetric weather or terrain cloud shadows; horizon, motion, weather and physical-device acceptance remain incomplete.
- The game explicitly calls the deduplicated `Sky.loadMoonSurface()` for a local 256x128 CC BY 4.0 map. It samples only the visible Moon disc, keeping phase/cloud compositing independent. Missing art leaves the plain disc and the caller logs the load error; startup/saves do not wait. `Sky` owns/disposes the texture and handles completion after disposal. The JPEG and adjacent attribution are mandatory offline-shell entries; Pause exposes the attribution. This adds an estimated 0.167 MiB of mipmapped RGBA storage, not geometry or an additional draw pass.
- `src/construction-occlusion.ts` identifies instanced floors, walls and frames between camera and character; individual obstructing pieces fade. Floors retain an opaque, depth-writing pass for unaffected instances and use a separate transparent pass only while a batch has faded instances; both share geometry and opacity attributes. The opaque floor batch still casts its ordinary shadow, including for faded instances. A fading batch submits its floor geometry twice, so large structures require profiling. `src/tree-occlusion.ts` transforms projected sightlines to the rebased scene before testing three center-body and two shoulder rays against nearby trees. Authored bark and foliage share localized shader clearance using conservative transformed bounds; this may activate without a triangle intersection, but only the sightline region is discarded and opaque/depth-writing state is retained. The shader wraps the foliage wind hook, so cleared coordinates include wind motion. Procedural fallback meshes use exact raycasts and smooth per-mesh fade. Materials restore independently; the opening can visually cut trunks and leaves shadow casting unchanged. Enclosed rooms shorten the follow camera. Floor/wall edges can still cross the player, and this does not constitute complete camera or building-geometry acceptance.
- `src/tree-harvest.ts` and `src/world.ts` track finite tree felling, falling, trunk sawing and separately collected logs by canonical resource ID. Tree size determines yield; active harvests survive terrain edits and chunk regeneration instead of becoming another resource. Falling is bounded animation, not world-wide physics. Mira shares the same resource state and carries an exact finite bundle; unloaded targets pause her work.
- `src/save.ts` validates version 1–13 save shapes before loading. Older versions receive browser-local backups before migration; version 11 adds Atlas state, version 12 persists canonical geographic actor positions, and version 13 adds tree-harvest stages and exact NPC carried quantities. Older saves derive missing geography and retain depleted resource IDs; `pre-timber-backup` protects earlier saves. New games must craft the axe before selecting it; the optional version-13 `axeRequiresCrafting` marker distinguishes those saves from legacy saves, which retain the formerly free axe without duplication and are backed up to `pre-equipment-backup` before migration. The starter saw remains freely available. `main.ts` validates structured snapshots before serializing IndexedDB writes so invalid state does not replace the previous save. Main-menu JSON export and validated import are available; import backs up the replaced primary slot. Enemies and projectiles remain transient.
- `src/pwa.ts` handles optional installation and user-approved update activation. `vite.config.ts` emits a versioned service worker which precaches a matching shell, hashed assets, bundled character models/textures, two terrain atlases and ten nature GLBs; saves remain in IndexedDB. The mature fir is a separate content-versioned, demand-cached local asset and does not increase the mandatory shell. Unavailable uncached art falls back to the existing fir sapling. Optional-cache mutations coordinate across worker generations using a separate IndexedDB lock and isolate legacy workers in another cache namespace; if the lock is unavailable, caching is best-effort and network responses remain usable. Wider regional asset management and multi-tab update coordination remain incomplete.
- `src/onboarding.ts` validates optional introduction state and derives compact objectives from real gather/Pack progress, modular floors and Mira recruitment. `main.ts` offers guided or free play, pause-menu skip/restart, and persists the optional state in current saves; older saves without it load in free exploration. Skip, restart and recruitment success confirmations await a committed save rather than promising persistence while it is still pending. This does not implement the future full survivor tutorial.
- `src/geography.ts` defines Earth-radius and equatorial-circumference constants, latitude/longitude normalization, haversine distance, great-circle bearing and forward movement, ECEF/tangent conversions, parallel transport of headings, hierarchical tile keys, climate zones and hemispheric season helpers. Forward movement and inverse tangent latitude use stable vector/`atan2` calculations for small polar offsets. Tangent inverse conversion selects the origin-facing hemisphere and rejects offsets beyond the planet radius; the far hemisphere cannot be distinguished from its orthographic projection. `src/planet.ts` adapts projected compatibility positions to canonical geography and provides a reusable `PlanetTangentFrame`; enemy collision uses it for geographically filtered nearby blockers. `main.ts` uses geographic movement for the player, rover, Mira and enemies; `src/wildlife.ts` uses it for animal movement and proximity. The frame is not yet integrated across entities, terrain and rendering.
- `main.ts` keeps gameplay/save coordinates unwrapped and applies a 1,024-meter scene-root render origin. Camera and terrain/build placement rays account for the origin; this is a rendering precision mitigation, not a fully separated global/local/render transform architecture.
- Roads and structures are moved to their nearest equivalent projected copies as the player travels; road models and attached paint markers follow those changes. `src/roads.ts` indexes road slots for linear-time connection refresh. This compatibility projection is not a canonical tangent-frame implementation.
- The World Atlas is rendered by `main.ts` on a procedural canvas. M or the minimap opens it; it uses hierarchical equirectangular scale levels, persistent compact explored-tile IDs, named map markers and straight-line distance/transport estimates. Atlas state is optional in version-11-and-later saves and remains render-compatible with older saves.
- `src/style.css` styles the interface; `public/` contains the PWA shell; `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` deploys the built Pages artifact.

This is not yet a multiplayer-ready simulation boundary or a complete spherical planet. Entity state, distant NPC/wildlife simulation, seam-aware navigation and polar gameplay require further separation and verification.

- `src/physics.ts` provides lightweight shared actor-movement and ray-obstruction queries for structural pieces and buildings. Enemy movement uses a local-tangent collision query for nearby blockers; player/vehicle collision and line-of-sight remain projected. `src/planetary-projectile.ts` integrates thrown stones and straight hostile shots in a player-centered runtime tangent frame, transports velocity when that frame relocates, and maps positions back to the projected scene, where existing obstruction and hit queries run. This does not make the scene a shared tangent frame. It avoids an external rigid-body dependency but does not implement full Rapier-style dynamics. Jump, gravity and water immersion state remain in `main.ts`.
- `src/runtime-frame.ts` owns the current tangent frame and projected render-origin conversion. Player, rover and nearby Mira, wildlife and enemies step through the tangent frame; distant actors retain geographic movement. Camera and terrain-picking conversions use its projected compatibility boundary. Object rendering, terrain meshes and most collisions still operate in the projected frame and must migrate together.
- Terrain chunks retain chunk-local sampling coordinates for their vertices and ground-cover instances separately from their render transforms. Resource and deposit x/z ownership is likewise kept separately from object transforms and shifts with compatibility rebases. Terrain edits, resource lookup and gathering use ownership coordinates; the remaining scene systems still depend on projected transforms.
- `World.setTangentFrame` and `ConstructionRenderer.setTangentFrame` provide staged local geometry paths for terrain, ground cover, resources and modular construction. They are **not enabled in gameplay**: actor/camera rendering, queries and collision must migrate together before switching them on. Chunk and construction reprojection currently runs synchronously and needs a bounded runtime scheduling strategy.
